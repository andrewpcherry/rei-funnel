The architecture

Six situations, six named reviews, four routing tiers

The roofer funnel proved the mechanic: name the situation, ask only the questions that situation needs, then hand back a specific next step instead of a generic quote. Seller motivation fragments harder than roof damage does, so this version adds conditional follow-ups, four disqualifying routes, and a tier the CRM can act on.

The four questions every branch still asks

  • Title. Owner, one of several owners, executor or power of attorney, family member helping, or a tenant. Anyone who cannot sign gets routed, not chased.
  • Occupancy. Owner, tenant, vacant, or someone else. Skipped on the landlord branch because it was already asked.
  • Listing status. Currently listed and under contract both route out. An expired listing routes in as a strong lead.
  • Location and timeline. Out of market ends the flow honestly instead of collecting a lead nobody will call.

What this changes downstream

  • Tier A fires an immediate call task. Owner, in market, not listed, inside 60 days, or a set legal date.
  • Tier B goes to a sequence. Real situation, unresolved blocker, usually heirs or a decision not yet made.
  • Tier C gets the guide and a slow nurture. Researching, more than 90 days out, or just curious.
  • Routed out never enters the pipeline. Tenants, out-of-area owners, and actively listed properties get a straight answer and an exit.
